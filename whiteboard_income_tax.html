WHITEBOARD: Why Ordinary Americans Don't Owe Income Tax

A 31-year tax refuser dismantles the legal machinery behind Form W-9, withholding certificates, and the statutory definition of a "U.S. person."

Dave Champion, author of Income Tax: Shattering the Myths, argues that the United States income tax is not legally imposed on ordinary American citizens earning domestic income. Drawing on 31 years of not filing or paying taxes, Champion uses a whiteboard to expose what he claims the IRS deliberately obscures: the true legal purpose of its forms.
Key Claims

What the Whiteboard Reveals

1

Form W-9 Is a Withholding Certificate

The speaker cites 26 CFR 1.1471-1 and 26 CFR 1.1441-1 to argue that Form W-9 is legally classified as a "withholding certificate" designed solely to identify payees in transactions involving foreign-owned income—not domestic wages.

2

"Withholding Agent" = Foreign Only

Citing 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(16) and 26 CFR 1.1441-7(a), he contends a withholding agent is strictly defined as someone with control over the income of a foreign person—giving them no jurisdiction over ordinary Americans.

3

"U.S. Person" = Pipeline, Not Citizen

The term "U.S. person" (26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(30)) was introduced in 1962 and, he argues, describes only an intermediary or pipeline moving U.S.-sourced income to a foreign owner—not an individual citizen.

4

The Pipeline Illustration

Using a whiteboard, he diagrams how a foreign investor routes funds through U.S. intermediaries to buy dividend-paying shares—Form W-9 exists, he claims, only to track who withholds tax before money goes offshore.

5

"Zero" Statutory Requirement for Domestic Income

The speaker concludes there is no statutory requirement for an ordinary citizen earning domestic income within the 50 states to file or sign any tax form—and that signing a W-9 under penalty of perjury is legally inappropriate for those outside the foreign-withholding pipeline.

The Speaker's Thesis on Income Tax and Form W-9

Champion opens by claiming that the IRS does not explicitly state the legal purpose of its forms because doing so would deter citizens from signing them. Drawing on his self-reported 31-year history of not filing or paying income taxes, he focuses on Form W-9 as a case study.

He argues that the form is legally classified as a "withholding certificate" under Treasury regulations—specifically citing 26 CFR 1.1471-1 and 26 CFR 1.1441-1—which he asserts are designed solely to establish whether a payee is a U.S. or foreign person in transactions involving foreign-owned income.

"The IRS doesn't tell you the legal purpose of the form because if they did, you wouldn't sign it."

Statutory Definitions of Withholding Certificates and Agents

To support the argument, the presenter analyzes several sections of the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) and Code of Federal Regulations (CFR):

  • Withholding Agent (26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(16) & 26 CFR 1.1441-7(a)): The speaker contends that a withholding agent is strictly defined as someone who has control or custody of the income of a foreign person. Consequently, he argues that withholding agents have no legal jurisdiction over ordinary Americans earning domestic income.
  • U.S. Person (26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(30)): The speaker claims this term was introduced in 1962 and is contextually used in tax law only to describe an entity acting as an intermediary or pipeline moving U.S.-sourced income to a foreign owner—not an individual citizen working a domestic job.
  • Withholding Certificate (26 CFR 1.1441-1): He argues that Form W-9 falls under this regulatory definition and therefore serves the foreign-income tracking system, not the domestic wage system most Americans believe it governs.

The Whiteboard Pipeline Illustration

Using a whiteboard diagram, Champion illustrates a hypothetical financial pipeline to explain his interpretation of the tax system:

The Scenario: A foreign investor (e.g., a Peruvian billionaire) routes funds through a U.S. investment firm or law firm to purchase dividend-paying shares in a domestic corporation (e.g., Apple).

  • The Pipeline: The domestic corporation acts as the origin of the income, paying dividends to the investment firm, which then passes the funds to the foreign owner.
  • The Withholding Process: Because the ultimate beneficial owner is foreign, the intermediaries (the investment firm or law firm) act as withholding agents. Champion claims Form W-9 is used within this pipeline to identify domestic intermediaries to the IRS.
  • The Purpose: The form, he argues, allows the government to track who is responsible for withholding the tax before the money goes offshore—not to tax the domestic worker earning a paycheck.

Form W-9 exists to identify who in the pipeline is the U.S. intermediary moving foreign-owned money—not to establish that a waitress in Ohio owes income tax.

— Dave Champion, Income Tax: Shattering the Myths

Claims on Domestic Tax Requirements

Champion concludes the tax portion of his presentation by asserting that there is "zero" statutory requirement in U.S. law for an ordinary citizen earning domestic income within the 50 states to file or sign any tax forms.

Champion argues that signing a W-9 under penalty of perjury is inappropriate for individuals who do not fit his defined criteria of a "U.S. person" in the context of foreign withholding. The form, he claims, is being used coercively on populations it was never statutorily designed to cover.
